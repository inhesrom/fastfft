The generic page-policy proposal has a legitimate memory-accounting rationale, but no established performance rationale yet. N210's4160-byte logical span uses8192 bytes (+96.92% initialized backing padding); N4096's36992 uses40960 (+10.73%). A separately justified maximum relative padding budget could limit over-allocation without per-N rules. However, choosing a budget specifically to exclude these two cases is threshold fitting, and a rule based on actual rounding overhead can switch backends non-monotonically near page boundaries. A minimum span derived from worst-case padding can be monotone: with64-byte logical granularity, maximum rounding waste is4032 bytes, so require span≥4032/epsilon for a predeclared overhead fraction epsilon. No independent epsilon is supplied by this ticket. This arithmetic does not establish that4KiB alignment is harmful, that the owners fit in an entire relevant cache working set, or that64KiB is a natural performance crossover. Page alignment is allocation alignment, not huge-page use. Retaining benefits at large sizes also needs validation; it cannot be assumed from their size alone.
