# Interrupted diagnostic clock review

Read-only review of the retained, missing-alignment diagnostic executable and its first interrupted order-coupled attempt. No benchmark or test was run by the reviewer.

The frozen `dependent_adds` begins at 0x2cdf60. The loop at 0x2cdf67–0x2cdf7b contains four dependent ADD-immediate instructions on RAX, DEC RCX, and JNZ. RCX is initialized to 10,000,000. The complete loop lies within one 32-byte block and one 64-byte block. All three calls from measure_ghz target this same function. There is no folded ADD chain, wrong loop bound, or per-call static alignment difference in the generated code. Attached disassembly includes the three-call max reduction.

There are 32 raw records including eight controls, hence 64 original clock endpoints. One endpoint is 2.8575090265138265; all remaining endpoints lie between 4.9968357537589325 and 5.053244138521045. The anomalous row is pow2:f32:inv:ip:n4096, zero-based process_index 6, Estimate constructed first. Both rows use identical Stockham AVX512-YMM 8x8x8 descriptions, pass correctness, and use 19 calls per timed batch. Estimate/Measure medians are 2690.894737/2720.421053 ns. The ending probe is 5.028492064599529.

Since the probe reports the maximum of three rates, every initial probe took at least 13.998206 ms. At the ending rate a probe takes about 7.954671 ms. Relative to that rate, the initial triple therefore incurred at least 18.130605 ms of excess duration. The cell wall time is 1.885462924 s, exceeding the other seven inverse-IP processes' median 1.858238533 s by 27.224391 ms. These are consistent with a disturbance concentrated around the initial probe, but do not identify its cause. One isolated pause affecting only one of three probe calls cannot explain the low maximum. All three must be affected, whether by separate off-CPU delays or persistently lower effective on-CPU throughput. Five-second quiet averages do not exclude such a short event.

The reported GHz is a throughput-derived estimate based on four nominal dependent-ADD cycles per iteration, not a direct frequency measurement. Source and existing results cannot distinguish true downclocking from descheduling, interrupt activity, or an on-CPU execution slowdown. Static loop alignment alone cannot explain the isolated process endpoint because the same binary and loop are used throughout.

Build provenance is independently wrong for the declared diagnostic protocol: order-coupled/env.json reports an empty compile-time rustflags field. Root confirmed acceptance and prescribed-repeat executables used -C llvm-args=-align-all-functions=6. This attempt remains invalid and retained; the missing flag is not evidence that it caused the transient clock outlier.

The smallest suggested instrumented control retains the ADD body, all three samples, max reduction, and existing clock gates, while recording every probe's wall duration, thread CPU duration, thread context-switch deltas, and endpoint CPU IDs. A large wall/thread-CPU gap supports off-CPU delay. Slow wall and thread-CPU durations together still require hardware cycles/reference cycles to distinguish frequency from reduced instructions per cycle. Thread CPU and switch query brackets extend slightly outside the wall bracket, so their deltas are diagnostic context rather than exact subtraction identities. No retry or warmup change was recommended.
